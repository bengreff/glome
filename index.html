What you are seeing

Slice view is the 3D cross-section of the 4D world through your eyes, the way a 3D visitor would see it. Turning toward ana sweeps the slice through the landscape. Lighting and shadows are computed in full 4D, so hills beside you in ana, outside the slice, still cast shadows into it.

Radar. The ground of a 4D world is three-dimensional, so its minimap is a ball, with you at the centre. The disc through the middle is exactly the ground your slice shows (the bright wedge is what is on screen); above the disc is ana, below is kata. Ground above 24 m is solid, so mountains float in the ball, and blue haze is water. On the disc, orange marks mountains lying toward ana from that spot and blue toward kata. The dot in the middle of your view and the ring on the disc are the same point.

The whole planet. Zoom all the way out and the ball holds the entire planet: its whole outer surface is a single point, your antipode, 785 m away by any straight walk. P pins the ball where you stand; walk and you move through it as a dot, and your slice becomes a curved sheet, a great 2-sphere through you and your antipode.

Compass. Unlike Earth, a 3-sphere can carry a compass with no poles: three perpendicular directions (i, j, k, after the quaternion units) defined everywhere. Walking straight keeps your compass heading fixed but slowly rolls the other two around it, once per lap of the planet.

Boulders are 4D balls. Your slice cuts each in an ordinary sphere whose size depends on how far its centre lies toward ana or kata, so as you turn they swell out of nothing, shrink and vanish. The radar shows them all and rings the ones your slice cuts. They are solid, with friction: push into one and you stop; hold Space and you climb its surface (which can carry you toward its centre in ana).

The ground is a 3-sphere: walk straight in any direction and you come back to where you started, about 1.6 km later. The planet spins in two planes at once, so there are no poles and the days never repeat.
