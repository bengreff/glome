What you are seeing

Slice view is the 3D cross-section of the 4D world through your eyes, the way a 3D visitor would see it. Turning toward ana sweeps the slice through the landscape. Lighting and shadows are computed in full 4D, so you will see shadows cast by hills that are beside you in ana and not in the slice at all.

Radar. The ground of a 4D world is three-dimensional, so its minimap is a ball. You are at the centre. The disc through the middle is exactly the ground your slice view shows (the bright wedge is what is on screen); everything above the disc lies toward ana, everything below toward kata. Height is drawn as nested shells: coast, 12 m and 24 m. Blue haze is water. Summits sit on stalks that drop to the disc, and your own trail shows how your walk has curved through all three ground directions.

Compass. Unlike Earth, a 3-sphere can carry a compass with no poles: three perpendicular directions (±i, ±j, ±k, after the quaternion units) defined everywhere. Walking straight keeps your compass heading fixed but slowly rolls the other two around it, one full turn per lap of the planet. M locks the radar to the compass instead of to your body, so you can watch your slice tilt when you turn toward ana.

The whole planet (press = past 180 m). Like a two-hemisphere world map, the planet is shown as two balls: everything within a quarter lap (393 m) of a pin, and everything around the pin's antipode. Leave either ball through any point and you enter the other through the opposite point. The pin starts where you started; R moves it to you. The dashed line is where walking straight ahead takes you, all the way round (dots every 100 m), and the triangles are the highest summits.

Triptych (V). A 4D creature's retina is three-dimensional: right, up and ana. Your slice is only its middle layer. The triptych shows three layers of that retina side by side, kata 25° · your slice · ana 25°, like peripheral vision.

The ground is a 3-sphere: walk straight in any direction and you come back to where you started, about 1.6 km later. The planet spins in two planes at once, so there are no poles and the days are irregular.
